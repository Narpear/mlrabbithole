## No “it’s easy to see that”

Machine learning has a way of hiding its hardest steps inside a sentence: *it’s easy to see that…* This is a place to slow down. We’ll start with a question, define every symbol, and follow each step until the answer feels earned.

The goal is not just to collect intuitions. It’s to know where they came from, when they apply, and what changes when an assumption does.

## A taste: adding noise

Here’s one small idea from diffusion models. We start with an image $x_0$, combine it with Gaussian noise $\epsilon$, and control the mix with a timestep $t$:

$$
x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}\,\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I).
$$

Move the slider. At the beginning, the image signal is all there is. As $t$ grows, the noise takes over.

<Callout type="warn" title="A variance detail worth keeping">
The second argument of $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$ is the variance, not the standard deviation. That is why the noise is multiplied by $\sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t}$.
</Callout>

That square root is a small symbol with a big job: it turns a variance into the standard deviation that scales the sampled noise. We’ll keep following details like this, one question at a time.
